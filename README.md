# European Energy & Commodity Risk Dashboard

A multi-page risk analytics app for European energy markets, built with Python and Streamlit. One app, three pages, each designed with a different reader in mind.

**[Live Demo →](https://energy-risk-dashboard-zj3n46fw8txggaj3su3br6.streamlit.app)**

| Page | Link | Designed with … in mind | Question it answers |
|------|------|-------------------------|---------------------|
| **Risk** | [/risk](https://energy-risk-dashboard-zj3n46fw8txggaj3su3br6.streamlit.app/risk) | a trading-house middle office (risk control) | How much can we lose, are we within limits, and does the VaR model hold up? |
| **Power** | [/power](https://energy-risk-dashboard-zj3n46fw8txggaj3su3br6.streamlit.app/power) | power trading desks and utilities | How much does a German gas plant earn over its fuel and carbon costs in the hours it runs, and how much can that margin move? |
| **Market** | [/market](https://energy-risk-dashboard-zj3n46fw8txggaj3su3br6.streamlit.app/market) | data and analytics firms | Which countries are exposed, what is the news mood, and is anything unusual? |

The sidebar holds the shared settings (commodity: TTF Natural Gas, WTI, Brent or EU Carbon; date range), and the selection carries across pages.

## Risk page

**Market risk of the selected commodity**
- **Volatility**: one function, `common.vol_summary`, writes every volatility statement on the Risk and Market pages and in the AI input. A neutral heading (no traffic-light colour) gives the 30-day volatility annualised with the daily figure in brackets, its multiple of the pre-war level (the median 30-day volatility of October to December 2025, the last full quarter before the strikes on Iran on 28 February 2026; a fixed window, like the fixed dates of the historical scenarios) and its percentile over the last 3 years; the line under it gives the pre-war level and the percentiles over the last year, 3 years and since the start of the data (2017, or 2018 where the series starts later), and says that the 3-year window includes the volatility after the war began. When the 30-day window contains contract roll days, a line under the heading lists them and gives the 30-day volatility with the new contract's return on those days (see Roll jumps). Below that, in small grey type, the 3-year label: Normal, Elevated (above the 75th percentile) or Extreme (above the 90th). The label always uses today's 3-year thresholds; when the end date is in the past, the label says so and names the months of that window, which then differs from the 3 years to the end date behind the percentiles. On 19 October 2026 TTF was 1.9 times its pre-war level and at the 53rd percentile of 3 years: ordinary for recent years, about twice the volatility before the war. It measures volatility, not overall risk. Every volatility in text, tiles and tables is written annualised first with the daily figure in brackets, and the volatility charts are annualised. Next to it: current and average volatility, the correlation with the comparison series since the start of the range and over the last 30 days, and the 1-day VaR 95% per unit of a long position.
- **Price Trends**: dual-axis chart with key policy and market events (Fit for 55, Russia's invasion of Ukraine, the EU oil embargo and its crude ban, Nord Stream, the EU gas price cap, the ETS reform with ETS 2, ETS extension to shipping, CBAM, the US-Israeli strikes on Iran on 28 February 2026 and the Qatar LNG halt and Hormuz closure on 2 March 2026).
- **Forward Curve**: monthly NYMEX contracts with a daily close within the last 5 trading days, at their latest close. Oil curves are labelled contango or backwardation; When the WTI or Brent front month has fewer than 3 business days left to its last trading day (exchange rule, holidays not modelled), the label gives the expiry date. TTF is shown as a seasonal curve, with a winter (Oct to Mar) or summer (Apr to Sep) average and the winter-summer spread only when the season has at least 3 monthly contracts. The TTF contracts are NYMEX Dutch TTF; the European benchmark is ICE Endex TTF.
- **Volatility & Correlation**: 30-day rolling volatility (daily) and rolling correlation of returns.
- **Cross-Commodity Correlation Matrix**: TTF, WTI, Brent and carbon, over the full period and the last 30 common trading days, each titled with its start and end date.
- **Value at Risk**: historical VaR 95%, VaR 99% and Expected Shortfall 97.5% on the last 250 trading days, with the full-period figures alongside, and the max daily loss with its date (for Brent, the page says when that date is a roll day and gives the worst day excluding roll days); return distribution, 60-day rolling VaR, and a 250-day backtest of the commodity's own VaR (exceptions, Kupiec, Basel traffic light).
- **GARCH(1,1) Forecast**: Student-t GARCH; the daily volatility forecast for day 5 and day 10 ahead, the 10-day holding-period volatility, and a simulated 90% range of future volatility for each day (500 paths resampling the standardised residuals with the fitted parameters held fixed; it is not a confidence interval for the parameters).
- **Market Regime**: per-commodity volatility thresholds: Normal, Elevated, Extreme. Past days are labelled with today's thresholds. The chart uses shades of one colour (light Normal, dark Extreme), not traffic-light colours, because the label is a single-window percentile, not a signal.

**Positions & Limits**
- Enter positions in €m notional (long or short) in TTF, WTI, Brent and carbon.
- **Book VaR 95% / 99% (€) and Expected Shortfall 97.5%** from the last 250 trading days.
- **Limit usage** against a user-set VaR limit (green < 80%, amber 80 to 100%, red = breach), with standalone VaR per position, the book's gross and net notional, and the diversification benefit.
- **VaR backtest** over the last 250 days: exceptions at 95% and 99%, **Kupiec** proportion-of-failures test, and the **Basel traffic light** (0 to 4 green, 5 to 9 yellow, 10 or more red), shown only when the backtest has the full 250 days.
- **Historical scenario replay**: the positions entered above, held unchanged through three past windows (Russia's invasion of Ukraine, the August 2022 gas peak, the first two weeks of the 2026 Iran war). For each window: the cumulative return of each commodity, the € P&L per position and for the book, and the book's worst single day. It replays what happened; it is not a forecast.

## Power page

- **German day-ahead power** (bidding zone DE-LU) from the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform, document A44, kept per hour (per 15 minutes since 1 Oct 2025). ENTSO-E publishes two price series for DE-LU: sequence 1 is the 12:00 Single Day-Ahead Coupling (SDAC) auction, sequence 2 is EXAA's separate 10:15 auction; only sequence 1 is used. If ENTSO-E cannot be reached, or no `ENTSOE_TOKEN` is set, the same prices come from the Energy-Charts API (Fraunhofer ISE, data from Bundesnetzagentur / SMARD); the page states which source each year came from. The two sources were compared price by price (all of 2024, 8,784 hours, and 2026 to date): identical. **Peak** is the time-weighted average over weekdays 08:00 to 20:00 (Berlin time), **baseload** over all 24 hours.
- **Timing.** A delivery day's power is sold in the day-ahead auction at 12:00 the day before, every day of the year (Monday's power on Sunday). Gas and carbon do not trade at weekends, so each delivery day is matched with the last TTF price and EEX EUA auction **before** it: Friday's prices for Monday. When the end date is today, tomorrow's delivery day is included once its prices are published (around 13:00); its gas price is then the latest TTF price Yahoo Finance has, which is today's price (not yet a settlement) once today's row has appeared and otherwise the previous trading day's close; the headline says which, and flags when gas is from an earlier day than carbon. That flag needs NYMEX Brent (`BZ=F`, the same trading calendar as `TTF=F`) to have a price for the day TTF lacks, so an exchange holiday such as US Thanksgiving is not reported as a late price. The headline names the TTF contract month used (the front month, not day-ahead gas).
- **Clean spark spread** = power − TTF / efficiency − (0.18406 / efficiency) × EUA, at 45%, 50% and 60% efficiency on a higher heating value (HHV) basis, as S&P Global Platts publishes German spreads; 50% is the central case. 0.18406 tCO2 per MWh of gas is Platts' 0.053942 tCO2e/MMBtu (HHV) converted at 3.412142 MMBtu/MWh. TTF is quoted on a gross calorific value basis, so an HHV emission factor is used; the often-quoted 0.202 tCO2/MWh is on a net calorific value basis and would overstate the carbon cost by about 10%. It is a gross margin per MWh, before start-up, other variable and fixed costs.
- Headline: the latest delivery day's peak, baseload and **flexible plant** spreads at 50% HHV, then a table of the peak and baseload spreads at 45%, 50% and 60% and the share of days with a positive peak spread over the last 12 months at each efficiency. The flexible plant runs in any period of a business day (all 24 hours) where the price is above its fuel and carbon cost; start-up costs, minimum run times and part-load operation are ignored, and the page shows how often it would start.
- Charts: the peak and baseload spreads over time (with the share of days on which the peak block averaged below baseload, because solar output lowers midday prices), and their decomposition into power price, fuel cost and carbon cost.
- **What a flexible plant earns**: the 400 MW flexible plant's gross margin per quarter next to the average peak spread of the quarter. The margin cannot be negative by construction (the plant runs in exactly the periods that cover its fuel and carbon cost, as a unit offering at that cost would be dispatched, and pays nothing to start, stop or stay on), so it is an upper bound; since prices became 15-minute on 1 Oct 2025, the finer periods alone raise it by a few percent against the same prices averaged to hours, and the page shows by how much; the page shows how often the plant would start and the order of magnitude of those costs at NREL's lower-bound median wear-and-tear cost per gas combined-cycle start (35 to 55 dollars per MW, 2011 dollars, excluding start fuel; Kumar et al., Power Plant Cycling Costs, NREL 2012, Table 1-1). Below it, the **gas-output-weighted price** (a capture price): the day-ahead price weighted by the actual output of German public-supply gas plants (Energy-Charts public net generation, fossil gas; industrial self-generation not included), compared with the peak and baseload averages over the last 90 days. It is a day-ahead capture price, not what the plants sold at: output sold forward earns the forward price instead. The 30-day comparison ends on the last day with complete generation data, which the page names.
- **Spread risk**, as day-on-day changes between consecutive delivery business days (Monday compares with Friday): the 30-day volatility and the 1-in-20 fall (95% historical VaR of the daily change) of the peak spread in €/MWh (not %, because power prices and the spread can be negative; for the same reason the Power page does not use the annualised volatility summary or the pre-war comparison of the Risk and Market pages), and the 1-in-20 fall in the flexible plant's daily margin in €. These measure how much the margin moves from one day to the next, not a loss on a position: each day's margin is fixed once the auction clears.
- **Buy next month's gas now, or in a week? (gas leg only)** `TTF=F` is matched to the monthly contract it tracks (e.g. November 2026 delivery). A zero-mean GARCH(1,1) with Student-t shocks, fitted on its daily log returns, gives a 90% range for the price in 5 business days; the gas volume is the month's business days × the flexible plant's recent average running hours × 400 MW ÷ 50%, also shown as the equivalent MW of the TTF month contract (which delivers the same MW in every hour of the month). The page shows the cost of the month's gas bought today, the range if bought in a week, and the extra cost at the 95th percentile. This is the gas leg only: a plant hedging its spread sells power forward at the same time, and power and gas prices move together (the page shows the correlation of their monthly averages over the last three years). The 90% range is rounded to 0.5 €/MWh, because it comes from simulated paths. A spot (day-ahead) gas comparison is not shown, because no freely licensed daily TTF spot series is available.
- **EUA price** from EEX primary-auction results (€/t, spot allowances), carried forward at most two weeks between auctions. Rows whose contract code contains "EAA" or "EUAA" (aviation allowances) are dropped; if two auctions fall on the same day their prices are averaged. If EEX cannot be reached, no spread is shown: no built-in or approximate carbon price is used.
- **Download CSV**: per delivery business day, peak and baseload power, TTF and its settlement date, EUA and its auction date, fuel and carbon cost at 50%, peak and baseload spreads at 45%, 50% and 60%, the flexible plant's hours, starts, average spread and daily margin, the day-on-day changes behind the risk figures, and the gas-weighted price.
- **Not included, because no freely licensed data is available:** coal (clean dark spread, API2 coal has no free source), EEX power futures (the public data is for personal use only) and TTF day-ahead gas.

## Market page

- **Country Exposure**: EU-27 member states, Norway, Switzerland, the UK and Turkey, every year from 2020 for which Eurostat publishes the EU-27 value (2020 to 2024 as of October 2026), all from the Eurostat API with no hand-entered value and no composite score. The question follows the commodity. Gas and oil: "Which countries are most exposed to imported gas (oil)?", ranked by net imports of the fuel as a share of all the country's energy = the fuel's share of gross available energy (nrg_bal_c) × its import dependency (nrg_ind_id), shown next to the raw share, the dependency, total energy import dependency and the renewable share, with the EU-27 value as reference. Carbon: "Which economies emit the most greenhouse gas per unit of output?", ranked by greenhouse-gas emissions per million euro of GDP at 2020 prices, with the renewable share. The page states what is not included (gas storage, supplier concentration, LNG import capacity; for oil, emergency stocks; for carbon, which emissions the EU ETS covers). Includes a bar chart of every ranked country, the Eurostat inputs of one country and their trend over the years (a value Eurostat does not publish, such as Turkey's renewable share, shows as "n/a", and no empty trend chart is drawn for it), and the tone of recent headlines that name the country or use its adjective.
- **Headline Tone**: headlines from the last 7 days from BBC Business, OilPrice and Google News (queries limited with `when:7d`), at most 4 per feed, duplicates across feeds removed, and the " - Publisher" suffix Google News appends removed before scoring. They are scored with FinBERT (ProsusAI/finbert via the Hugging Face Inference API) when it is available, otherwise with FinVADER. FinBERT needs a Hugging Face token with inference credits; without a working token FinVADER is what runs, and the page shows one short line with the reason (for example "FinBERT unavailable (Hugging Face inference credits exhausted); FinVADER used."), never the provider's error text. If no feed returns a relevant headline, no tone is computed (the page never scores placeholder text). Every feed keeps only headlines containing one of the selected commodity's keywords (whole words); for TTF the headline needs an explicit natural-gas term (natural gas, LNG, TTF, gas storage, gas supply, gas pipeline, Gazprom, Equinor and similar) or the word "gas" with a European place name, because "gas prices" in US English means petrol. Dropped for every commodity: US retail fuel stories ("gas prices" with gallon, AAA, pump, gasoline or the US Energy Secretary), US-domestic stories with no link to Europe (US LNG export news is kept), and pages that are not news (exchange product pages such as "Financial Overview" or "Trade Ideas", bare report titles such as "Oil Market Report - October 2026"). Dollar signs in headlines are shown literally. Both models are trained on company news (FinBERT on Financial PhraseBank), so the score is the tone of the wording, not a price direction. FinBERT's weights are licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial).
- **Headline Tone, Last 30 Days**: Google News headlines of the last 30 days, de-duplicated, filtered with the same rules and scored with FinVADER; days are Berlin calendar days; the 30-day average is over all headlines, and the number of headlines per day is shown under the daily bars.
- **Statistical Checks**: each check is calibrated on the commodity's own last 3 years (the same 750 trading days as the regime thresholds): 30-day volatility of daily returns above its 95th percentile (spike) or below its 5th (unusual calm); GARCH 10-day forecast more than 30% above or below the 3-year average 30-day volatility, with the GARCH model for this check fitted on the same last 750 trading days (fitted on the whole selected range, which includes the 2021-22 crisis, the model's long-run level sits above the 3-year average, so model and reference would describe different periods; the Risk page still fits its GARCH on the selected range, so the two forecasts can differ); 30-day correlation with the comparison asset outside its 5th to 95th percentile range; a daily loss beyond 2× the 3-year 99% VaR on the latest trading day (the table also gives the date of the last such loss in the last 252 trading days and how many there were, so a loss months ago is shown as a past event, not as firing now). For Brent, roll days (when Yahoo's continuous `BZ=F` switches contract under the ICE expiry rule) are excluded from the loss check and its VaR99, and the page names any excluded day; for TTF a flagged day on or just after a front-month expiry is marked as possibly partly a roll (not verified); for WTI roll days are not identified. A table shows every check, its threshold, the latest value and on how many of the last 252 trading days it fired, so the base rate of each flag is visible.
- **AI Risk Interpretation** (optional): the statistics on this page summarised in three sentences by Claude via the Anthropic API. It sees only those statistics, not fundamentals such as storage, flows or the forward curve. The prompt gives every threshold with the current value and the side it is on, and asks for neutral wording on the GARCH forecast (its direction from current volatility and its position against the 3-year average). The prompt also forbids calling GARCH implied or option-based, presenting a past check as a current signal, and giving trading or position-sizing advice. Before display, a fixed rule drops any sentence that tells the reader to watch whether a value pushes above (or falls below) a level it is already above (below).
- **Data Export**: CSV downloads of commodity risk data, headline tone, and the country table with the raw Eurostat inputs and the EU-27 reference row.

## Methodology Notes

**Carbon benchmark.** KEUA, the original EUA ETF, was liquidated in March 2026. The app now tries `CARB.L` (WisdomTree Carbon ETC, USD line on the LSE), then `KRBN`, then `ICLN`, and labels the charts from whichever resolves. `CARB.L` tracks ICE EUA futures, but its quote is an ETC share price in USD, not €/tCO2, so its returns also carry EUR/USD moves, roll yield and fees. The Power page uses EEX auction prices in €/t instead.

**Volatility.** The 30-day volatility is the equal-weighted standard deviation of simple daily returns over 30 trading days (at least 20), shown annualised (daily x √252) with the daily figure in brackets. This is a simplification: RiskMetrics uses an exponentially weighted average (λ = 0.94 for daily data) of log returns. The average volatility on the page covers the selected date range, while the regime thresholds use the last 3 years, so the two reference periods differ.

**VaR.** Historical simulation, 1-day horizon. The 95% VaR is the 5th percentile of daily returns, the loss exceeded on 5% of days (about 1 day in 20), and the 99% VaR the loss exceeded on 1% of days; neither is the worst case. Percentiles use linear interpolation between the two nearest days (numpy's default). Expected Shortfall 97.5% is the average of the returns at or below the 2.5% percentile: with 250 days that is the worst 7 days (about 2.8% of the sample, since the interpolated 2.5% point falls between the 7th and 8th worst day). The headline figures use the last 250 trading days; the full-period figures (which include the 2022 crisis on the default range) are shown alongside. On the Risk page's position block, VaR and ES come from the last 250 days of the book's € P&L. Prices run up to and including the end date chosen in the sidebar; Saturday and Sunday rows (Yahoo's Sunday evening session) are dropped, and the last value can be a price from the current or the evening electronic session, not the settlement, so it can change within the hour. Each price series is downloaded once (from 2017) and shared by the volatility, the percentiles, the thresholds and VaR, so they all use the same last price.

**Roll jumps.** `TTF=F`, `CL=F` and `BZ=F` are Yahoo Finance's continuous front-month futures. On the day the front contract rolls, the series switches to the next contract and that day's return is not a real price move. Example: ICE Brent stops trading on the last business day of the second month before delivery, so the November 2026 contract expired on 30 September 2026. `BZ=F` closed at 103.53 dollars a barrel that day (November) while the December contract closed at 98.03; on 1 October the series followed December, which closed at 102.31. The series showed a fall of 1.18% that day while the December contract rose 4.37%: the 5.50 dollar gap between the two contracts turned a rise into a fall. Roll days are not removed, so they can add to the volatility, the tails, the max daily loss, VaR and backtest exceptions; the Risk page says so. For Brent the page identifies roll days from the ICE rule (the first trading day of each month, and the last trading day of December, because ICE moves the December expiry one business day earlier before New Year's Day) and says when the max daily loss falls on one. For the latest 30-day volatility the Risk page checks every day of the window against the individual monthly contracts on Yahoo Finance, downloaded together with the continuous series: a roll day is a day on which the continuous close equals one contract's close while on the day before it did not. Under the volatility heading it lists those days, with the series' return and the new contract's return, and gives the 30-day volatility with the new contract's own return on them; the 3-year label and the percentiles still use the unadjusted figure. Expired contracts are no longer listed on Yahoo Finance, so a day on which the continuous close matches no single available contract is written as not identified, not guessed. For Brent an unmatched day counts as a roll day only if the ICE rule above makes it one. Elsewhere on the page (max daily loss, VaR, backtest) TTF and WTI roll days are not detected: Yahoo's switch date for those series does not always match the exchange expiry. For TTF the page flags a max daily loss that falls on or just after a front-month expiry (ICE Endex: trading stops two UK business days before the delivery month; UK holidays are not modelled), without claiming the move is a roll. WTI settled at −37.63 dollars on 20 April 2020; a percentage return is not defined across a negative price, so those returns are left out and the page says so.

**VaR backtest.** The Risk page backtests the selected commodity's own VaR per unit of exposure. The position block's backtest is hypothetical: today's positions are applied to past returns, and each day's VaR is estimated only from the 250 days before it (no look-ahead). Kupiec's test also rejects a model with too few exceptions (over-conservative). The Basel traffic light (green 0 to 4, yellow 5 to 9, red 10 or more exceptions at 99%, BCBS 1996, https://www.bis.org/publ/bcbs22.pdf) is defined for 250 days; with a shorter backtest the page shows the exceptions and the Kupiec test but no traffic light, rather than scaling the thresholds.

**GARCH(1,1).** Fitted with Student-t shocks, since daily energy returns have fat tails. α captures reaction to shocks, β persistence; α + β near 1 means volatility shocks fade slowly. The page shows the daily volatility forecast for day 5 and day 10 ahead (the volatility of a single day's return that far out, not of a 5-day or 10-day period) and the 10-day holding-period volatility, the square root of the sum of the 10 daily forecast variances. The forecast reverts to the long-run volatility √(ω / (1 − α − β)), but for these commodities α + β sits at 0.97 to 0.99, where the formula divides by almost zero and swings from day to day. The reference level is therefore always the sample average 30-day volatility; the GARCH long-run value is shown for information only when α + β < 0.98 and it is within 1.5× the sample average. On the Market page the GARCH check compares the 10-day forecast with the 3-year average 30-day volatility (the regime window), not the sample average.

**Stale prices.** In a run of 5 or more identical closes (a suspended or illiquid quote), the first day is kept and the repeats after it are treated as missing, and no return is computed across the gap. The Risk page lists the excluded periods.

**Historical scenarios.** The stress test replays three past windows with the positions entered in Positions & Limits; it shows what happened, not a forecast or a probability. Each position is entered at the close before the window and held unchanged (no rebalancing, hedging or stop-loss), so its P&L is the position × the instrument's cumulative return, and the book's worst day is the largest one-day change in the value of the positions inside the window. The returns are the page's own daily returns (Yahoo continuous front-month futures for TTF, Brent and WTI, roll days included; for carbon the `CARB.L` ETC in USD, so the carbon return includes the EUR/USD move). An instrument with returns on fewer than 70% of a window's trading days is reported as no data. The windows are fixed dates, independent of the sidebar range:
- **Russia invades Ukraine**: close of 23 February 2022 to close of 8 March 2022. The invasion began on 24 February (European Council conclusions of 24 February 2022); 8 March is the day the US announced a ban on Russian oil imports and Brent's highest close of the episode on Yahoo (127.98 dollars); TTF's highest close was on 7 March.
- **August 2022 gas peak**: close of 29 July 2022 to close of 26 August 2022, the record TTF front-month close (about 339 €/MWh) after Gazprom announced a three-day Nord Stream 1 shutdown (Quantum Commodity Intelligence, 26 August 2022, https://qcintel.com/article/winter-ttf-gas-trades-at-record-352-mwh-goldman-says-market-overshoots-8125.html).
- **2026 Iran war, Qatar LNG halt and Hormuz**: close of 27 February 2026 to close of 13 March 2026, the first two trading weeks after the US-Israeli strikes on Iran on Saturday 28 February. QatarEnergy halted LNG production on 2 March and an IRGC official said that day that the Strait of Hormuz was closed (CRS report R45281, 11 March 2026; QatarEnergy statement reported by Marhaba, 4 March 2026). TTF rose 39% on 2 March and fell 16% on 10 March (Trading Economics, 10 March 2026: "declined more than 15% to below €50/MWh").
Each window contains a Brent roll day (1 March 2022, 1 August 2022, 2 March 2026), when part of the Brent move is the gap between contracts. With the default book (TTF +€10m, Brent −€5m, carbon +€3m) the book P&L is +€11.61m, +€8.56m and +€3.44m, and the worst days are −€4.37m (25 February 2022), −€0.66m (23 August 2022) and −€1.96m (10 March 2026); an independent recalculation from raw Yahoo prices gives the same figures.

**Regime detection.** Per-commodity thresholds only: Elevated starts at the 75th and Extreme at the 90th percentile of the commodity's own 30-day volatility over the last 3 years, 750 trading days (the selected range if that history is too short); the page also gives today's percentile rank. There is no industry-standard cut-off for a volatility regime; desks quote where volatility sits in its own history, which is what the percentile shows. A longer history would include the 2021-22 crisis and set the "normal" line too high. A lower volatility never gets a higher regime. The Statistical Checks caption on the Market page and the AI input use the same summary. The Risk page caption shows the thresholds used. The regime chart and statistics label every past day with today's thresholds, so labels for earlier years are applied with hindsight, not what a desk would have seen at the time.

**Event lines.** Each date on the Risk page's price chart was checked against a primary or contemporaneous source: Fit for 55 presented 14 July 2021 (EERA); Russia's invasion 24 February 2022 (European Council conclusions); EU oil embargo (sixth sanctions package) adopted 3 June 2022, with crude by sea banned from 5 December 2022 after the wind-down and pipeline crude exempt (Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer sanctions tracker); Nord Stream explosions 26 September 2022, "serious sabotage" (CNBC, 18 November 2022); EU gas market correction mechanism in force 1 February 2023 and able to apply from 15 February 2023 (ACER, 1 February 2023); European Parliament adopts the ETS reform including ETS 2 on 18 April 2023 (EP press release; the Council adopted it on 25 April); EU ETS covers shipping since January 2024 (European Commission); CBAM definitive regime from 1 January 2026 (European Commission, DG TAXUD); US-Israeli strikes on Iran 28 February 2026 and the IRGC statement that Hormuz was closed on 2 March 2026 (CRS R45281, 11 March 2026, https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-03-11_R45281_70874465f4435fd92357ac85f4af8f89300419a0.html); QatarEnergy LNG halt 2 March 2026 (Marhaba, 4 March 2026, https://marhaba.qa/qatarenergy-temporarily-suspends-lng-production-and-select-downstream-products/; Business Today, 2 March 2026); ETS 2 start postponed to 1 January 2028 (ICAP; formally adopted by the Council on 5 March 2026; outside the data range, so not drawn).

**Clean spark spread caveats.** Power is the day-ahead price, while gas is the TTF front-month future, so the tenors don't match; S&P Global Platts computes its German day-ahead spreads from its own TTF day-ahead assessments, which are not freely available. Efficiencies are on an HHV basis (50% HHV is roughly 55% on a lower heating value basis). German plants buy gas at the THE hub, which trades at a spread to TTF; like Platts, the page uses TTF. Fuel cost excludes gas transport and other variable costs, as in the Platts spreads. The peak block includes public holidays, as exchange peak products do. `TTF=F` is a continuous front-month series that jumps at each monthly roll and follows the NYMEX (US) trading calendar.

**Country exposure.** No composite score and no weights. Gas and oil: net imports of the fuel as a share of all gross available energy = (gross available energy of the fuel ÷ gross available energy of all products, Eurostat nrg_bal_c, nrg_bal GAE, siec G3000 or O4000XBIO and TOTAL, ktoe) × import dependency of the fuel (nrg_ind_id, net imports ÷ gross available energy of the fuel). A country that imports all its gas but uses little gas (Sweden) therefore ranks low. Dependency is shown as published: below 0% means a net exporter (Norway), counted as 0 in the product; above 100% means imports also refilled stocks. The EU-27 values match Eurostat's "Energy statistics, an overview" (2024: natural gas 20.6% of gross available energy, 85.0% imported; oil 37.8%, 96.6% imported). Gross available energy includes international maritime bunkers, which raises the oil share of bunkering hubs such as Malta, the Netherlands and Belgium. Carbon: total greenhouse-gas emissions excluding LULUCF (env_air_gge, TOTX4_MEMO) ÷ GDP in chain-linked volumes (nama_10_gdp, CLV20_MEUR), in tCO2e per million euro at 2020 prices, so a fall over time is not an effect of inflation; levels across countries use 2020 exchange rates, not purchasing power. Renewable share: nrg_ind_ren (REN). Not included: gas storage, supplier concentration, LNG import capacity, emergency oil stocks, and which emissions the EU ETS covers. Countries without a value for an input of the measure in the selected year are listed as not ranked. Data are cached for a day; the page shows the date of each dataset's last Eurostat update.

### Optional API keys

Without keys the app still runs: sentiment falls back to FinVADER, the AI summary is hidden and power prices come from Energy-Charts.

- **FinBERT**: create a token at [huggingface.co/settings/tokens](https://huggingface.co/settings/tokens) (a fine-grained token needs the "Make calls to Inference Providers" permission) and set `HF_TOKEN`. If FinBERT still is not used, the Market page shows a short reason (for example token missing or invalid, no Inference permission, rate limit).
- **AI Risk Interpretation**: set `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`.
- **ENTSO-E power prices**: request a security token in your ENTSO-E Transparency Platform account (My Account, Web API Access) and set `ENTSOE_TOKEN`. Without it the Power page uses Energy-Charts, which carries the same day-ahead prices.

On Streamlit Cloud, add them under Settings → Secrets (never put tokens in code); locally, set them as environment variables.
